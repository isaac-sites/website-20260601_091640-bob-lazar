## Introduction

The 2024 review by the U.S. Department of Defense’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) did not simply revisit old UFO claims. It also tried to untangle a long-running naming problem that has shaped public understanding of the Pentagon’s involvement with unidentified aerial phenomena. For years, the labels [AAWSAP]({{ 'aawsap/' | relative_url }}) and AATIP were often treated as interchangeable, both in media reporting and within parts of the UFO community. AARO argued that this blurred important distinctions between a formally funded Defence Intelligence Agency programme and a later, looser network of officials who continued discussing UAP cases after that programme ended. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

That distinction matters when assessing figures connected to the programme ecosystem, including [Kit Green]({{ 'how-credible-is-kit-greens-uap-story/' | relative_url }}). The mere fact that someone was described as “AATIP-linked” does not automatically establish what role they had, what records they produced, whether they worked under an official government contract, or whether they were involved only in later informal discussions. AARO’s review narrowed some claims while leaving others disputed, particularly around how the AATIP label evolved after the original contract was cancelled. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

## How AARO defined AAWSAP and AATIP

The central finding of AARO’s historical review was that there was a real Defence Intelligence Agency programme funded through a contract awarded to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), but that the programme’s naming history became muddled over time. According to AARO, the DIA established the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) in 2009. The report acknowledged that official documents sometimes used AAWSAP and AATIP interchangeably, creating confusion that persisted for years. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

AARO then added a qualification that became one of the most discussed passages in the report. It stated that, unlike AAWSAP, AATIP was never an official Department of Defense programme in its own right. After AAWSAP ended, AARO said that some officials used the AATIP label for an informal UAP-focused community of interest inside the Department of Defense. According to the review, this activity had no dedicated budget, no formally assigned staff, and no recognised programme status. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

This was not merely a semantic correction. It changed the meaning of claims that had circulated since the 2017 public revelations about a “secret Pentagon UFO programme”. If AATIP after AAWSAP was primarily an informal continuation of interest rather than a funded programme, then references to “AATIP” can point to very different things depending on the date, context and source. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

The confusion was amplified because official records themselves were inconsistent. AARO acknowledged that AAWSAP and AATIP appeared interchangeably in some documentation. That means the naming problem cannot be dismissed as a purely public misunderstanding; it also existed within the documentary record that researchers later tried to reconstruct. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

### Why some researchers dispute AARO's framing

The AARO interpretation is influential because it comes from the Pentagon's designated UAP office, but it is not universally accepted.

Researchers who have examined programme documents and statements from former participants argue that AATIP functioned as an umbrella designation while AAWSAP referred more specifically to the contract vehicle through which much of the work was funded. They point to Pentagon correspondence and later statements from programme participants suggesting that the relationship between the two names was more complex than a simple official-versus-unofficial divide. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.academia.edu/121609473/On_the_AAWSAP_AATIP_Confusion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">On the AAWSAP AATIP Confusion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) On the AAWSAP/AATIP Confusion30 Jun 2024 — The research clarifies that AATIP was the overarching program name, while AAWSAP...</span></span></span>

Some former insiders have also described the naming issue as partly driven by security and bureaucratic concerns. Accounts from individuals associated with the programme suggest that alternative labels were sometimes used in different contexts, contributing to later confusion about whether the names referred to the same activity, overlapping activities, or different phases of the same effort. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.academia.edu/121609473/On_the_AAWSAP_AATIP_Confusion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">On the AAWSAP AATIP Confusion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) On the AAWSAP/AATIP Confusion30 Jun 2024 — The research clarifies that AATIP was the overarching program name, while AAWSAP...</span></span></span>

The result is not a settled historical consensus. AARO's review established the current official Pentagon position, but debate remains over whether that framework fully captures how participants themselves understood the programme during its operation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why the name changed public perception

The distinction became especially important after the December 2017 reporting that introduced AATIP to a mass audience.

Public discussion quickly attached the AATIP name to Navy UAP videos, Luis Elizondo, claims of hidden government knowledge and broader questions about extraterrestrial technology. In popular coverage, AATIP often became shorthand for a Pentagon UFO investigation programme. The more technical and bureaucratic AAWSAP label remained far less familiar. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Aerospace_Threat_Identification_Program" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification ProgramApril 20, 2026 — According to the Department of Defense, the AATIP ended in 2012 after fi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2026</span></span></span>

AARO's review effectively argued that this public narrative compressed several different stages into one story:

* The funded DIA contract and research effort generally associated with AAWSAP.
* The production of technical studies on advanced aerospace concepts and related subjects.
* Investigations that expanded into UAP and paranormal topics.
* Informal post-cancellation discussions and case reviews that some participants continued under the AATIP name.

## What the label can and cannot prove about Kit Green

For assessing Kit Green, the AATIP identity problem is significant because it limits what can be inferred from association alone.

Green's most concrete connection to the AAWSAP/AATIP environment comes through biological-effects research that was produced within that broader DIA-funded ecosystem. Publicly available records show his involvement with subjects that interested programme participants, particularly alleged physiological effects associated with anomalous encounters. That is a documented connection. What is less clear is any claim that Green held a central command role in an official Pentagon programme called AATIP. The public record does not establish that. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024 — 8 Mar 2024 — established AAWSAP in 2009, which was also known AATI...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

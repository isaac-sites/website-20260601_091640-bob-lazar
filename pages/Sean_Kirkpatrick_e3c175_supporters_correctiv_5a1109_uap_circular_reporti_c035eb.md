## Introduction

One of the central arguments made by [supporters]({{ 'supporters-0a40e4/' | relative_url }}) of Sean Kirkpatrick is that some of the most persuasive UAP narratives gain credibility through repetition rather than through genuinely independent evidence. In this view, the problem is not necessarily widespread deception. Instead, stories can acquire the appearance of confirmation as they circulate between officials, former officials, journalists, researchers, podcasts, congressional staff, and online communities before returning to public debate as seemingly corroborated claims.

Kirkpatrick repeatedly described this process as a form of circular reporting. As director of the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), he argued that some allegations about hidden crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering programmes appeared to trace back to a relatively small network of interconnected sources rather than multiple independent lines of evidence. For supporters, identifying this mechanism was one of his most important contributions because it offered an explanation for why sincere witnesses can reinforce a narrative without necessarily proving it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — AARO assesses that the inaccurate claim that the USG is reverse-engin...</span></span></span>

## What circular reporting means in UAP claims

Circular reporting occurs when a claim appears to be confirmed by several sources, but those sources are ultimately drawing from the same original information stream.

In ordinary journalism and intelligence analysis, investigators try to establish whether reports are truly independent. If five people repeat the same rumour, that is not equivalent to five independent witnesses. The distinction matters because apparent corroboration is often treated as evidence.

Applied to UAP claims, the pattern can look like this:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A person hears a story about a secret programme.
2. They share it with colleagues or researchers.
3. The story appears in books, interviews, podcasts, or briefings.
4. New listeners encounter the claim from multiple places.
5. Those listeners then cite the widespread discussion as evidence that the claim must be true.

</div>

At that point, the story can appear heavily supported even if the underlying source trail remains narrow.

Kirkpatrick argued that many high-profile allegations about hidden government possession of non-human technology displayed exactly this characteristic. In a 2024 article after leaving AARO, he described the narrative as a "textbook example of circular reporting", where individuals repeatedly relayed information that ultimately traced back to the same small group of people.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_M._Kirkpatrick" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sean M. ## How classified hints and secrecy amplify the loop

The UAP field contains features that make circular reporting especially difficult to detect.

Many claims involve classified programmes, compartmented access, or alleged activities that cannot easily be checked by outsiders. When details are unavailable, participants often rely on reputation, authority, and second-hand accounts.

This creates a powerful feedback mechanism:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A former official says they heard about a classified programme.
* Another source confirms hearing similar rumours.
* Researchers interpret the overlap as corroboration.
* Journalists report that multiple insiders have heard the same story.
* Public audiences see several apparently independent confirmations.

</div>

Yet the overlap may exist because everyone ultimately heard the same rumour from the same original source.

Supporters of Kirkpatrick argue that secrecy can unintentionally strengthen this effect. If records are inaccessible and direct witnesses remain unavailable, the distinction between evidence and repetition becomes harder to maintain. What survives in public discussion is often not the original event but the growing number of references to it.

This concern was reflected in AARO's historical review, which stressed that investigators attempted to trace allegations back to named programmes, locations, individuals and documents rather than treating repeated claims as independent confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3575588/aaro-director-dr-sean-kirkpatrick-holds-an-off-camera-media-roundtable/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro director dr sean kirkpatrick holds an off camera media roundtable</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Director Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick Holds an Off-Camera...31 Oct 2023 — These reports will be used to inform AARO&#x27;s cong...</span></span></span>

## When media repetition starts to look like evidence

The rumour loop becomes more persuasive when information crosses between different communities.

A story might begin in a private UFO research network, appear in a podcast interview, be discussed on social media, reach journalists, enter congressional discussion, and then return to the UFO community as evidence that government officials are taking the allegation seriously.

At each stage, the story appears to gain legitimacy.

Supporters of Kirkpatrick argue that this process can create an illusion of mounting evidence. A congressional inquiry, for example, may demonstrate that lawmakers are investigating a claim, but it does not necessarily verify that the claim is true. Likewise, extensive media coverage may show public interest rather than factual confirmation.

The distinction is important because many UAP debates revolve around allegations of secret crash-retrieval programmes that are difficult to verify directly. AARO's position was that investigators must separate the existence of a rumour from evidence supporting the rumour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3575588/aaro-director-dr-sean-kirkpatrick-holds-an-off-camera-media-roundtable/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro director dr sean kirkpatrick holds an off camera media roundtable</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Director Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick Holds an Off-Camera...31 Oct 2023 — These reports will be used to inform AARO&#x27;s cong...</span></span></span>

## The AARO assessment of the rumour loop

AARO's 2024 historical report made the circular-reporting issue unusually explicit.

The report stated that claims that the United States government was secretly reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology were, "in large part", the result of circular reporting among individuals who believed such programmes existed despite the absence of supporting evidence. AARO said that it examined specific allegations and found no verifiable evidence that the named programmes represented extraterrestrial reverse-engineering efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3575588/aaro-director-dr-sean-kirkpatrick-holds-an-off-camera-media-roundtable/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro director dr sean kirkpatrick holds an off camera media roundtable</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Director Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick Holds an Off-Camera...31 Oct 2023 — These reports will be used to inform AARO&#x27;s cong...</span></span></span>

The office further stated that many alleged programmes either did not exist, had been misunderstood, or were actually legitimate national-security activities unrelated to extraterrestrial technology. According to the report, authentic classified programmes identified during the review had been reported through established congressional oversight channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">wikisource.orgPage:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/94 May 2024 — AARO assesses that the inaccurate claim that the USG is...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span><span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3575588/aaro-director-dr-sean-kirkpatrick-holds-an-off-camera-media-roundtable/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro director dr sean kirkpatrick holds an off camera media roundtable</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Director Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick Holds an Off-Camera...31 Oct 2023 — These reports will be used to inform AARO&#x27;s cong...</span></span></span>

For Kirkpatrick's supporters, these findings were significant because they represented an attempt to follow claims back to their origins rather than measuring credibility by popularity or repetition.

## Why supporters saw this as a corrective

Supporters generally do not argue that circular reporting disproves every extraordinary UAP claim.

Instead, they argue that it identifies a common failure mode in the discussion.

Under this interpretation, a witness can be honest yet mistaken. An official can possess a clearance yet still lack direct knowledge. Multiple people can repeat a claim in good faith while unknowingly relying on the same source network. AARO itself stated that many individuals appeared to be repeating allegations without an intent to deceive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3575588/aaro-director-dr-sean-kirkpatrick-holds-an-off-camera-media-roundtable/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro director dr sean kirkpatrick holds an off camera media roundtable</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Director Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick Holds an Off-Camera...31 Oct 2023 — These reports will be used to inform AARO&#x27;s cong...</span></span></span>

This perspective also explains why Kirkpatrick's defenders often describe him as a corrective rather than a debunker. They argue that his focus was not primarily on proving that every unusual sighting had a conventional explanation. Rather, it was on demanding independent evidence, documented chains of custody, and verifiable first-hand testimony before accepting claims of hidden non-human technology.
